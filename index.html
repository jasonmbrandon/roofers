Roof anatomy

What’s actually
under the shingles.

A roof is a system layered over your house, not one sheet of tabs. Pick a layer to see what it does — and what happens to the rooms below when it’s skipped.

The weather layer

Laminated tabs with a mineral surface, nailed in overlapping courses so water runs over every joint instead of into it. They shed the rain and take the sun; everything below is there for when they can’t.

The second line

A synthetic sheet rolled over the whole deck. It keeps the house dry while the shingles go on, and keeps it dry again when wind lifts a tab or drives rain sideways.

What you stand on

Plywood or OSB. Soft, delaminated or rotten boards get cut out and replaced. A new roof laid over a bad deck is a leak with a warranty on it.

Where damage starts

A self-adhering waterproof strip along the bottom edge, where ice dams and wind-blown rain do their worst. Cheap on the day, expensive to add later.

The last lip of metal

The lip at the eave that throws water clear of the fascia and into the gutter. Without it, water tracks back under the shingles and rots the trim instead.

Where leaks hide

Metal where the roof meets a wall, a chimney or another plane. Most “mystery” leaks we open up turn out to be flashing, not shingles.

The roof breathes here

The cap covers the peak; the vent under it lets the attic push out its hottest, wettest air. Seal it shut and the moisture condenses on the underside of the deck.

The exit

Sized to the roof it drains and pitched to its outlet. Wrong size or wrong pitch and the water goes to the foundation instead — which is a basement problem, not a roof one.

How a roof goes on

Four passes.
No shortcuts.

  1. 01

    Strip & inspect

    Old shingles come off, then every board is walked and sounded. Soft or delaminated deck is cut out before anything goes back on.

  2. 02

    Deck & membrane

    Underlayment rolled and lapped the right way, an ice-and-water strip along the eave, and the drip edge set before a single shingle lands.

  3. 03

    Shingles & flashing

    Courses laid from the eave up, straight and evenly exposed. Valley metal and step flashing go on before the shingles that cover them.

  4. 04

    Ridge & clear-up

    Ridge vent and cap close the top. Then the magnet sweep, the gutters cleared of nails, and a walk-round with you before we leave.

The job, start to finish

Five steps,
and you know all of them.

Nobody likes a contractor who disappears behind a ladder. Here is the whole sequence, including the parts where you get to say no.

  1. 01

    Talk

    Tell us what’s happening: a stain on the ceiling, tabs in the yard after wind, or a roof you already know is finished. A phone photo helps before we come out.

  2. 02

    Inspect

    We go up, photograph what we find, and check the deck, the flashings and the attic side — where the water actually shows up.

  3. 03

    Estimate

    A written, itemised number: materials, labour, tear-off, disposal, and anything that has to be fixed before the roof can be. No number over the phone.

    Build

Straight answers

The questions
we get asked most.

How long does a roof take?

It depends on the size, the pitch, how many layers have to come off and how much deck repair we find once the old roof is gone. The schedule is written on the estimate, and we tell you the same day if it changes.

What does a new roof cost?

Price comes from the size, the pitch, the layers to tear off, the condition of the deck and the material you choose. We measure the roof and give you a line-item estimate rather than a per-square number over the phone.

Will you work with my insurance company?

Yes. For storm damage we document what we find with photographs and measurements, and give you a written report your adjuster can work from. We don’t negotiate your claim for you, and we don’t invoice you for work the insurer hasn’t agreed to.

Can you fix part of it instead of replacing everything?

If a repair is the honest answer, we’ll say so — a section repair, a flashing rebuild or a set of ridge caps. We’ll also tell you when a repair is throwing money at a roof that’s at the end of its life.

Do you cover the roof if it rains while you’re working?

The roof is never left open. We strip and close the same day, and if weather turns we tarp and secure before we leave the site.

What if something goes wrong after you’ve gone?

Call us. Workmanship issues come back to us, and the manufacturer’s warranty covers material defects. Both are written on the paperwork we leave with you.

Are you insured?

Ask for the certificate at the inspection and we’ll email it before any work is scheduled. It should be the first thing you ask any roofer.
